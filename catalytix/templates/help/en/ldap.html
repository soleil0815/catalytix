With Administration → Active Directory everyone signs in with their domain account (Windows AD, Samba AD or OpenLDAP – there are preset buttons for each). Sign-in via the directory service is currently {{ "active" if f.ldap_on else "not active" }}.

  1. Connection: Server (e.g. ldaps://dc1.school.local, several separated by commas), Security (LDAPS or StartTLS recommended), Base DN.
  2. Service account with read access (Service account (bind DN) and password). Without a service account, enter the UPN suffix instead.
  3. TLS certificate: Fetch certificate from DC retrieves the certificate including the root CA. Check the fingerprint, then Trust & save. If you address the DC by IP, switch off Verify hostname.
  4. Who may do what? Enter the names of directory groups per role, one per line. Load groups lists the directory groups to pick from. The highest matching role wins; anyone in no group is rejected. Group DNs or OUs also work instead of names – recommended for administrators and librarians, because a plain name matches every group of that name anywhere in the directory (the buttons under Load groups insert the DN for these roles).
  5. Resolve nested groups counts memberships via subgroups (including the primary group such as Domain Users).
  6. Test: Test connection and Test sign-in show the DN, all groups (direct, primary, nested) and the resulting role – without saving first. Then Save.

Role, name, email and linked groups are updated at every sign-in. Running sessions are also re-checked every 4 hours using the service account: disabled or no longer authorised accounts are signed out.{% if f.ldap_on and not f.ldap_service_account %} Without a service account (as now) this re-check is not possible.{% endif %} If the directory is unreachable, users stay signed in.

AD sync and people cache ({% if f.ldap_on and f.ldap_cache %}active{% else %}currently off{% endif %}, service account required): in the background – hourly by default, Sync now starts it immediately – all AD people who may use the library are fetched. The person search at the desk then finds them instantly. Existing AD accounts are updated along the way (once a day without the cache), even if someone never signs in: name, email, role, groups and a sign-in name changed in AD (the account is recognised by the fixed AD identifier; loans move along). People who are disabled, deleted or no longer entitled in AD drop out of the cache; their account is locked (not deleted – loans stay visible) and unlocked again when the person returns. The user list then shows no longer in AD. If a sync would lock more than 30 % of the accounts, it locks none and reports it – usually the base DN or filter is wrong. An account locked by hand is never unlocked by the sync.

Local accounts always take precedence – the local admin account works even if the directory is down. The service account password is stored encrypted.
