Under Settings → Security → Two-factor authentication you decide who needs a second factor – separately for access from outside and from the internal network.

  • Mandatory for access from outside (internet): nobody, administrators, librarians and administrators, or everyone. Currently: {% if f.mfa_policy == "all" %}all users{% elif f.mfa_policy == "librarians" %}librarians and administrators{% elif f.mfa_policy == "admins" %}administrators{% else %}nobody (optional){% endif %}. Those affected are guided through setup after their next sign-in.
  • On the internal network – currently {% if f.mfa_internal == "skip" %}no two-factor prompt{% elif f.mfa_internal == "optional" %}not required{% else %}same as outside{% endif %}: same as from outside (same rules), not mandatory (users who have 2FA are still asked) or no two-factor prompt (password is enough).
  • Internal network: one network per line, e.g. 192.168.10.0/24. {% if f.internal_networks_set %}Your own networks are set.{% else %}Currently empty – all private networks count as internal, including Docker networks. Better restrict it to your real LAN.{% endif %}

Behind a reverse proxy the visitor's real address from X-Forwarded-For counts (Nginx Proxy Manager, Caddy and Traefik send it by default). If it is missing, access is treated as external for safety; forged headers from the internet are ignored. The bottom of the section shows how your current connection is classified – to check, open it once on a phone over mobile data: it must say “external”.

Own internet connection: when internal computers open the public domain, the router often passes them on with your public IP. Enter your fixed IP or DynDNS name here (re-resolved every 5 minutes).{% if f.own_public_set %} An entry is currently set.{% endif %} Caution: guest Wi-Fi also arrives via this address and then counts as internal. A cleaner solution is an internal DNS entry (split DNS).

A session started internally without a second factor is not valid from outside – a new sign-in is needed there, and “Stay signed in” has no effect.

Anyone signed in on the LAN without a second factor who has 2FA set up must confirm the code before changing factors or making security-relevant changes in the administration (users, approvals, resetting 2FA, locks, settings, Active Directory, email, backups). Branding and groups work without confirmation.
