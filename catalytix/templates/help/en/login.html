Sign in with your username and password.{% if f.ldap_on %} If you have an account on your institution's network, these are the same credentials you use to sign in to your PC.{% endif %} Stay signed in keeps you signed in on this device even after the browser is restarted; without it, the sign-in ends with the browser. Don't tick it on shared or other people's devices. It is not available for admins and, depending on the settings, from outside.

After a longer time without activity you are signed out automatically – the administration decides how long. Usually a notice appears two minutes before; one click on Keep working is enough.

If you have set up a second factor, a second step follows: enter the code from your authenticator app, click Confirm with security key, or use one of your emergency codes via Use a recovery code.

If it doesn't work:

  • “Incorrect username or password” – check the spelling and Caps Lock. For security reasons the page does not reveal whether the account exists or is locked.
  • “Too many failed attempts” – after 8 failed attempts sign-in is locked for 5 minutes. Wait, or ask an administrator to lift the lock.
  • Forgotten password – there is deliberately no “forgot password” function. {% if f.ldap_on %}For institution network accounts, IT resets the password; for local accounts, an administrator sets a new one.{% else %}An administrator can set a new password for you.{% endif %}
  • Lost phone or key – sign in with a recovery code, or ask an administrator to reset your two-factor authentication.
  • Security key not accepted – it only works at the address where it was registered. Otherwise use the app or a recovery code.

  • Suddenly asked for a second factor? Rules on the internal network are more relaxed than from outside. If you take your laptop home, you have to sign in again there – with your second factor.
